# Choosing a Forecaster

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user needs help choosing a forecaster, comparing forecaster types (recursive vs direct, single vs multi-series), or understanding which skforecast class fits their problem.

### Related skills

- **Next**: `forecasting-single-series` (apply the chosen forecaster to one target series)
- **Next**: `forecasting-multiple-series` (apply the chosen forecaster to several series jointly)
- **Next**: `foundation-forecasting` (configure `ForecasterFoundation` and the pre-trained models)
- **Next**: `baseline-forecasting` (build and benchmark a naive baseline with `ForecasterEquivalentDate`)
- **Next**: `statistical-models` (ARIMA or ETS as a reference for one monthly or quarterly series)
- **Next**: `backtesting-configuration` (the folds shared by every model of the comparison)
- **Next**: `autocorrelation-and-lag-selection` (analyse the series dynamics before configuring `lags`)
- **Next**: `feature-engineering` (build the input feature set: calendar, rolling, exogenous)

## Overview

Skforecast is a **machine learning forecasting library**: one workflow
(forecasters, backtesting, hyperparameter search, prediction intervals) for any
model, foundation models included. For a new problem, do not pick one model:
compare two forecasters against a seasonal baseline.

- **A gradient boosting forecaster** (`ForecasterRecursive`, or
  `ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries` for several series, with LightGBM): the model
  you train on your data. You shape it with your own variables and knowledge of
  the problem, explain it and tune it.
- **A foundation model** (`ForecasterFoundation` with Chronos-2,
  `autogluon/chronos-2-small`, Apache-2.0): no training. It is usually the more
  accurate of the two before any tuning.
- **A seasonal baseline** (`ForecasterEquivalentDate`): keep a model only if it
  beats it. For one monthly or quarterly series, add a statistical model
  (`ForecasterStats` with ARIMA or ETS) as a second reference.

For a new problem, compare these forecasters on the same folds and keep what beats the baseline. They are two candidates with different roles, not a ranking.

Pass the same `TimeSeriesFold(refit=True, fixed_train_size=False)` to every backtesting function, so that every model uses all the data before each fold: `backtesting_stats` refits every estimator except `Sarimax` in each fold and `backtesting_foundation` always forecasts from the history up to the fold, so with `refit=False` only the model you train would forecast from old data. `backtesting_foundation` warns that `refit` does not apply to it: pass `suppress_warnings=True` there.

| Your data | Forecasters to compare | Baseline |
|:--|:--|:--|
| A number, one series | `ForecasterRecursive` with LightGBM and `ForecasterFoundation` with Chronos-2 | `ForecasterEquivalentDate`: the value one season before |
| A number, one monthly or quarterly series | The same two, and `ForecasterStats` with ARIMA as a reference | `ForecasterEquivalentDate`: the value one season before |
| A number, several series | `ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries` with LightGBM and `ForecasterFoundation` with Chronos-2 | `ForecasterEquivalentDate`, one per series |
| A category | `ForecasterRecursiveClassifier` with a LightGBM classifier | The category one season before (`y.shift(season)`) |

If you know variables in advance (holidays, promotions, prices), pass them as `exog` to the forecasters. ARIMA is run without them.

Chronos-2 is `FoundationModel("autogluon/chronos-2-small")` (Apache-2.0, no training). LightGBM is the model the user trains and shapes with their own variables.

Example settings per frequency so that a first script runs (they are not tuned):

| One value per | `freq` | `season` (offset of the baseline) | `steps` | `lags` | Minimum history (values) |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|:--|--:|
| Hour | `h` | 24 | 24 | `[1, 2, 3, 24, 48, 168]` | 336 |
| Day | `D` | 7 | 14 | `14` | 90 |
| Week | `W` | 52 | 8 | `[1, 2, 3, 4, 52]` | 104 |
| Month | `MS` | 12 | 6 | `12` | 36 |
| Quarter | `QS` | 4 | 4 | `4` | 24 |

Evidence (26 public datasets, MASE, every model using all the data before each test period, one vote per dataset, LightGBM not tuned):

| Data | Chronos-2 had the lower error | LightGBM did worse than the baseline |
|:--|--:|--:|
| One series, nothing known in advance | 14 of 16 | 7 of 16 |
| One series, variables known in advance | 5 of 7 | 1 of 7 |
| Several series | 8 of 10 | 2 of 10 |

Chronos-2 did not do worse than the baseline in any dataset. With an automatic search of 20 trials, LightGBM had a lower error than Chronos-2 in 7 of 33 cases. In the 8 monthly and quarterly datasets with one series, ARIMA had a lower error than LightGBM in 6. Domain features and knowledge of the problem are not measured: on a given dataset a trained model can still win.

## Step 1: What Is the Target?

| Target | Go to |
|--------|-------|
| **A number, 1 series** (with or without exogenous variables) | Step 2a |
| **A number, several series** to forecast at the same time | Step 2b |
| **A category** (e.g., low/medium/high) | `ForecasterRecursiveClassifier`. It handles one series: with several, fit one classifier per series |

## Step 2a: Single Series

| Scenario | Forecaster | Why |
|----------|------------|-----|
| **A model trained on your data** | `ForecasterRecursive` | One model, recursive multi-step. Works with any sklearn-compatible estimator (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, RandomForest, etc.). Supports lags, window features, calendar features, exog, differentiation, transformers, weight functions, and all probabilistic prediction methods (bootstrapping, conformal, quantiles, distributions) |
| **A pre-trained model, no training** | `ForecasterFoundation` | Wraps pre-trained foundation models. `fit()` only stores the recent history as context. Strong accuracy without any tuning, also with a short history; needs its own backend (PyTorch for Chronos-2) and more compute to predict. Use `autogluon/chronos-2-small` (Apache-2.0) unless there is a reason not to: the weights of TimesFM 3.0, Moirai-2, TabPFN-TS, TS-ICL and Timestral have licenses that restrict commercial use. See the `foundation-forecasting` skill |
| **Horizon-dependent patterns** (e.g., predicting at 1h vs 24h requires different relationships) | `ForecasterDirect` | Trains one independent model per step: no error propagation. Better when the predictive relationship changes significantly across the forecast horizon. Requires `steps` at init; parallelizable with `n_jobs` |
| **Statistical reference** | `ForecasterStats` | Wraps ARIMA, SARIMAX, ETS, ARAR. For a monthly or quarterly series it is a strong reference: it often beats an untuned ML model. On daily data it added nothing in our tests |
| **Naive baseline** | `ForecasterEquivalentDate` | Predicts using equivalent past dates (e.g., same weekday last week). Every comparison needs it. See the `baseline-forecasting` skill |

## Step 2b: Multiple Series

| Scenario | Forecaster | Why |
|----------|------------|-----|
| **One model trained on all your series** | `ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries` | One global model learns cross-series patterns. Supports DataFrame or dict input (dict allows series with different date ranges). Encoding options: `'ordinal'` (default), `'ordinal_category'`, `'onehot'`, `None`. Supports per-series transformers, per-series differentiation, series_weights |
| **A pre-trained model, no training** | `ForecasterFoundation` | Forecasts every series with the same pre-trained model. `fit()` only stores context. Native quantile intervals. Chronos-2, TimesFM 3.0, TabICL, TabPFN-TS, TFC-T0, Nori, TS-ICL, Timestral and TiRex-2 support exog; TimesFM 2.5 and Moirai-2 do not. Same license note as in Step 2a. See the `foundation-forecasting` skill |
| **Other series are features for one target** | `ForecasterDirectMultiVariate` | All series become input features to predict a single `level`. Per-series lags via dict (`{'sales': [1,7], 'price': [1]}`). One model per step: no error propagation |
| **Naive baseline** | `ForecasterEquivalentDate` | It takes one series: backtest one per series and average the metric. See the `baseline-forecasting` skill |

`ForecasterRnn` (Keras RNN/LSTM/GRU, one model for all steps and levels, conformal
intervals only, requires keras) is also available: see the `deep-learning-forecasting` skill.

## Decision Flowchart

```
What is the target?
│
├─► A category ──► ForecasterRecursiveClassifier (one per series)
│                  Baseline: the category one season before (y.shift(season))
│
└─► A number
    │
    ├─► 1 series ──► compare, with the same TimeSeriesFold:
    │   ├─► ForecasterRecursive (LightGBM): the model you train
    │   │   └─► Step-specific patterns across the horizon? ──► ForecasterDirect
    │   ├─► ForecasterFoundation (Chronos-2): no training
    │   ├─► ForecasterEquivalentDate: seasonal baseline
    │   └─► Monthly or quarterly data? ──► add ForecasterStats (ARIMA, ETS)
    │
    └─► Several series
        │
        ├─► Forecast ALL series ──► compare, with the same TimeSeriesFold:
        │   ├─► ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries (LightGBM): the model you train
        │   ├─► ForecasterFoundation (Chronos-2): no training
        │   └─► ForecasterEquivalentDate, one per series: seasonal baseline
        │
        └─► Use other series AS FEATURES for one target
            └─► ForecasterDirectMultiVariate
```

## Key Comparisons

### Recursive vs Direct (Single Series)

| Aspect | ForecasterRecursive | ForecasterDirect |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Models trained | 1 | N (one per step) |
| Error propagation | Yes (predictions feed into next step) | No (each step uses only observed data) |
| Forecast horizon | Flexible (any `steps` at predict time) | Fixed at init (`steps` required) |
| Training speed | Fast (one model) | Slower (N models, parallelizable via `n_jobs`) |
| Memory | Lower (1 estimator) | Higher (N estimators stored in `estimators_` dict) |
| Best for | Most cases, especially short-to-medium horizons | Long horizons where patterns change per step |
| Prediction intervals | Bootstrapping + conformal | Bootstrapping + conformal |

### RecursiveMultiSeries vs DirectMultiVariate (Multiple Series)

| Aspect | ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries | ForecasterDirectMultiVariate |
|--------|-------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Goal | Forecast **all series** with one model | Use all series as **features** for one target |
| Input | DataFrame or dict of Series | DataFrame (all series as columns) |
| Target | All series (selected via `levels`) | One series (specified via `level`) |
| Strategy | Recursive (1 model, predictions feed back) | Direct (1 model per step, no error propagation) |
| Series identification | Encoding: `'ordinal'`, `'ordinal_category'`, `'onehot'`, `None` | All series create separate lag columns |
| Series with different ranges | Yes (via dict input) | No (all must share same range) |
| Per-series lags | No (same lags for all) | Yes (dict: `{'sales': [1,7], 'price': [1]}`) |
| Series weights | Yes (`series_weights` param) | No |
| Per-series transformers | Yes (`transformer_series` dict) | Yes (`transformer_series` dict) |
| Differentiation | Per series (`differentiation` as int or dict) | Same order for all series (`differentiation` is an int) |

### ML Forecasters vs Statistical Models

| Aspect | ML Forecasters | ForecasterStats |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Primary role** | The model you train and shape | Reference to compare against |
| Best for | Complex patterns, many exogenous features, several series | One monthly or quarterly series, interpretability, parametric intervals |
| Estimators | Any sklearn-compatible (LightGBM, XGBoost, RF, etc.) | Arima, Sarimax, Ets, Arar |
| Lags / window features | Full support (`lags`, `RollingFeatures`) | No (model handles its own structure) |
| Differentiation | Built-in (`differentiation` param) | Handled within model (e.g., ARIMA `d` parameter) |
| Exogenous variables | Full support | Arima, Sarimax and Arar (not Ets) |
| Prediction intervals | Bootstrapping (binned residuals) + conformal | Built-in parametric (`predict_interval` with `interval` or `alpha`) |
| Tuning | `grid_search_forecaster`, `random_search_forecaster`, `bayesian_search_forecaster` | `grid_search_stats`, `random_search_stats` |
| Backtesting | `backtesting_forecaster` / `backtesting_forecaster_multiseries` | `backtesting_stats` |
| Missing values in the series | Yes (NaN-tolerant estimator or `dropna_from_series=True`) | No (`ValueError`) |
| Feature selection | `select_features` / `select_features_multiseries` | Not applicable |

## Measured Comparison

The figures behind the two-candidate rule are in the table of "Where to start"
above. Their limits: the LightGBM of the study is untuned or lightly tuned. A
model with features built from knowledge of the problem can still win on a
given dataset, and accuracy is not the only criterion (explainability, cost at
scale, dependencies).

The user guide [Comparing forecasting models](https://skforecast.org/latest/user_guides/comparing-forecasting-models.html) evaluates the seasonal naive, Ridge, LightGBM, Chronos-2, AutoARIMA, AutoETS and an LSTM with the same backtesting on three datasets: one series with exogenous variables, a short monthly series and 50 daily series that share the same patterns. Use it to show users how each family behaves (accuracy, interval coverage and computation time) and as a template to compare candidates on their own data with the same `TimeSeriesFold`.

## Next Steps

Once the first comparison runs, work on the model you train:

1. **Define your problem**: 1 series: `ForecasterRecursive`; several series: `ForecasterRecursiveMultiSeries`; in both cases next to `ForecasterFoundation` and a baseline
2. **Choose an estimator**: LightGBM (`LGBMRegressor`) is the usual ML candidate: fast, handles categoricals and missing values. Untuned, it does not always beat the seasonal naive, so check it against the baseline
3. **Add features**: `RollingFeatures` (rolling mean, std, min, max) in `window_features` and `CalendarFeatures` in `calendar_features` (no manual exog needed for the calendar)
4. **Exogenous variables**: ML forecasters need the future values of `exog` at predict time, so use only variables known in advance (holidays, prices, weather forecasts). Chronos-2 also accepts variables known only up to today; in our tests they gave no consistent gain
5. **Handle non-stationarity**: Use the `differentiation` parameter instead of manual differencing
6. **Evaluate with backtesting**: `backtesting_forecaster` + `TimeSeriesFold` for realistic multi-step evaluation
7. **Tune hyperparameters**: `bayesian_search_forecaster` (Optuna-based), which can include `lags` in the search space
8. **Add prediction intervals**: `predict_interval(method='conformal')` or `method='bootstrapping'`. Both use in-sample residuals by default, which tend to give intervals that are too narrow: store out-of-sample residuals with `set_out_sample_residuals` and pass `use_in_sample_residuals=False`. See the `prediction-intervals` skill
9. **Keep the references**: `ForecasterEquivalentDate` always (one per series with several series; for a categorical target, the category one season before, `y.shift(season)`), and for monthly or quarterly data `ForecasterStats` with automatic ARIMA, `Arima(order=None, seasonal_order=None, m=12)`: without `m` it does not search seasonal terms
